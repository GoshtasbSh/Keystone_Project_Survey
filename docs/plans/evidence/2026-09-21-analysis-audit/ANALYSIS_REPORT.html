How the KeyStone survey analysis works, and what we checked

Prepared 21 September 2026 for the KeyStone Heights / High Ridge Estates research team · code revision 76f57db

This report documents the Survey Results section of the KeyStone dashboard: which survey question feeds every chart and statistic, exactly how each number is calculated, and the checks we ran to confirm the calculations are right. It also records three defects we found and corrected.

Three real defects were found, and all three are fixed. Apart from those, every statistic reads the column it should, the arithmetic is correct, and a text export and a numeric export of the same survey now produce identical results.

Before you quote any figure from the dashboard today

The Analysis panel displays a stored snapshot computed at the last data upload, so it does not yet include these corrections. As of 2026-09-21T05:24Z it still reports mold at 79.1%; the households' own answers give 33.3%. The figures update when the survey data is next reprocessed.

1. The three defects we found

Each of these changed numbers that appear in the Analysis panel. None of them altered any respondent's answer — they were errors in how the answers were read.

A1  Mold prevalence was more than double the real figure

Survey question: “Is there any evidence of mold in the following spaces? (Check all that apply)”

What went wrong. The question lists only places — Kitchen, Bathroom, Bedroom, Ceiling, and so on — plus a free-text Other: box. It offers no "no mold" option. So households with no mold answered by ticking Other: and typing "None", "No" or "No mold". The dashboard treated any answer at all as mold, so those households were recorded as having mold.

How many households. 34 of 84 households in the May export (28 of 75 in April) — every one of them had explicitly said they have no mold.

Effect on the results. Reported mold prevalence 73.8% instead of 33.3%. Each affected household also gained +30 on the IAQ score — a third of that whole score — which raised its Overall Risk by about 10 points.

The correction. A household counts as having mold only when it names an actual place, or describes mold in the Other box ("closet", "I can smell mold but haven't found it"). An answer that denies mold, or says the respondent does not know, is not counted.

A2  Households with air conditioning were scored as having none

Survey question: “What type of cooling system do you use and how old is it? (check all that apply)”

What went wrong. This is a grid: the rows are the four system types, and the answer chosen in each row is its age — "less than 10 years", "10 to 15 years", "More than 15 years", or "Don't know/Not applicable". The dashboard treated any non-blank row as "I have this". On the "No Air-conditioning" row that is wrong: a household that does have A/C answers "Not applicable" there, and was then scored as having no air conditioning.

How many households. 35 households took the no-A/C penalty; 29 of them had reported a real age for their central or window A/C, so they clearly have it.

Effect on the results. +4 on the IAQ score for 29 households that should not have had it, and the cooling charts showed an age where a system type was expected.

The correction. A row counts as "I have this system" only when it gives a real age band. The no-A/C penalty applies only when the household reported no A/C anywhere. A hand-written label table that claimed this question had no age dimension — it does — was removed.

A3  Three questions could not be read from a numeric export

Survey question: “House condition (QID141), affordable-housing urgency (QID17), mobile-home skirting (QID100)”

What went wrong. In Qualtrics these three questions have two answer options sharing one export code, because options were added after the export codes were set. A numeric export therefore could not say which option a code meant, and the dashboard showed the bare number.

How many households. On the May export: 61 of 75 households for urgency, 71 of 75 for skirting.

Effect on the results. Two charts showed "1" and "2" instead of answers, and those two questions behaved differently depending on which export format was uploaded.

The correction. Where two options share a code, the answer is the one Qualtrics' own settings deliberately assign to that code, rather than the one that lands there only by default. This was checked against the data, not assumed: for the 75 households present in both the text and numeric exports the pairing is one-to-one with no exception. Both formats now read identically. The lasting fix is to correct the export codes in Qualtrics.

2. How each number is calculated

The three component scores are each on a 0–100 scale and are combined into the Overall Risk score. The point values below are the dashboard's model, applied identically to every household.

Health score (0–100)

Five symptom questions are scored by how often the household reports them — weekly = 4, monthly = 3, seasonally = 2, annually = 1, never or rarely = 0 — then weighted:

Health = min( (Headache×0.5 + Respiratory×1.0 + Asthma×1.0 + Wheezing×0.8 + Tiredness×0.3) ÷ 14.4 × 80 , 80 )

and +20 (capped at 100) if anyone in the home visited a hospital or health centre for respiratory problems in the last year. The divisor 14.4 is the maximum possible weighted total, so the five symptoms alone can reach 80 and the hospital visit supplies the remaining 20.

The percentage charts

The two matrix blocks — the 11 home-improvement interventions and the 10 lived experiences — are reported as a percentage of households. An answer counts as positive when it is affirmative (“Like”, “Yes”); answers that decline (“Dislike”, “No”, “Not Applicable/Unsure”, “Prefer Not Answer”) and blanks do not.

Every percentage in this report uses all mapped responses as its denominator, not just those who answered the question. A household that skipped a question, or chose "Prefer not to answer", counts in the denominator but not the numerator. This makes the percentages directly comparable across questions, but it means each one is a share of the whole surveyed population rather than a share of respondents to that question. Read "42% would like improved drainage" as "42% of surveyed households said so", not "42% of those who answered".

4. What we did to check it

Every column verified twice, independently

For all 56 inputs we confirmed the column the dashboard reads by two separate means: the Qualtrics question ID in the export's metadata row, and the verbatim question text in the export's own header row. All 56 agree. Not one input fell back to guessing a column position.

The two export formats now agree exactly

Qualtrics can export answers as text (“Single Wide Mobile Home”) or as numbers (“1”). We have one export of each: April 15 in text form and May 4 in numeric form, sharing 66 of the same households. We ran the full analysis on each, restricted both to those same households, and compared every statistic.

161 statistics compared · 0 differences — the format the data is exported in no longer changes any result. Before the A3 correction, 8 statistics differed.

Every score recalculated by hand

We recomputed the Health, IAQ, Structural and Overall Risk scores for 75 households using a separate program that shares no code with the dashboard: it reads the CSV with a different reader, decodes each answer against the Qualtrics survey definition directly, and applies the arithmetic written out above. An error in the dashboard could not hide by being repeated, because the two were written independently.

75 of 75 households match exactly — every score, every risk band.

Automated tests

The corrections are pinned by tests that state the intended reading household by household, so a future change that reintroduces one of these defects fails immediately rather than quietly shifting an average. The suite is at tests/test_iaq_score_semantics.py, tests/test_qsf_label_resolution.py and tests/test_popup_output_is_clean.py; 171 tests pass.

Nothing was changed on the live dashboard

This audit read the live dashboard and ran calculations locally. No survey data or field data was uploaded, replaced or deleted. Counts were identical before and after: 110 survey responses, 16 field points, 321 community contacts.

5. What a reader should keep in mind

The dashboard shows a stored snapshot, not a live calculation. Every number in the Analysis panel was computed when the survey data was last uploaded. The corrections described here are deployed, but the panel will keep showing the old figures — including mold at 79.1% — until the survey data is reprocessed through Update Data. Nothing in this audit changed, moved or deleted any stored data.

Three questions still have ambiguous export codes in Qualtrics. QID141, QID17 and QID100 each have two answer options sharing one export code. The dashboard now resolves them correctly for this survey, and the resolution is confirmed against 75 households answering in both formats. But it is a repair, not a guarantee: if a respondent ever chooses one of the retired options it will be read as the other. Correcting the Recode Values in Qualtrics removes the risk.

Risk-score weights are a modelling choice, not a measurement. Overall Risk = 0.35 x Health + 0.35 x IAQ + 0.30 x Structural, and the individual point values (mold +30, each water problem +7.5, single-wide +25, and so on) are assumptions built into this dashboard. They are applied consistently to every household, so comparisons and rankings are sound, but the absolute scores are only meaningful relative to each other.

"Symptom prevalence" means at least seasonally. The respiratory, asthma and wheeze percentages count households reporting symptoms weekly, monthly or seasonally. A household answering "annually" is not counted in those percentages, though it does add to the Health score.

6. Recommended fixes in Qualtrics

Two problems are in the questionnaire itself. The dashboard now handles both, but correcting them at source makes the data unambiguous for anyone who analyses it, in any tool.

  1. Add a “No mold” option to the mold question. It currently lists only places, so respondents without mold have to write “None” in the free-text box — which is what caused defect A1. An explicit option removes the ambiguity for good.
  2. Correct the Recode Values on the house-condition, housing-urgency and mobile-home-skirting questions. Each has two answer options sharing one export code. Setting each option to its own code makes numeric exports unambiguous.
  3. Consider splitting the cooling question. Asking system type and system age in one grid means the “No air conditioning” row has no meaningful age, which is what caused defect A2.
